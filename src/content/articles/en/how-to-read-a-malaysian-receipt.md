# How to read a Malaysian receipt, line by line

I used to throw receipts away. RM10 for lunch, RM15 for petrol, RM8 for a drink. Each one felt too small to matter. Then I checked my spending for the week and RM200 was gone, and at month end my wallet was empty and I could not say where it went. It was harder because my money sat in different banks and e-wallets.

That is why I started looking at receipts properly. A receipt tells you exactly where the money went. You just have to be able to read it.

## The top: who and when

You will see the shop name, address and the date and time. A business registered for SST often shows its registration number here too. The date matters when you track spending, because the day you paid is the day the money left.

## The middle: what you bought

Each line is an item, the quantity, and the price. Quantity forms vary: `2 x 5.50`, `x2`, or just two lines. Check that the line price times the quantity makes sense.

## The money block at the bottom

This is the part people skip, and the part that explains why the total is higher than the items.

| Line | What it is |
|---|---|
| Subtotal | All items added up, before charges |
| Service charge | The restaurant's own charge, usually 10% |
| SST | Tax, 6% for food and drink |
| Rounding | Adjustment to the nearest 5 sen, on the total only |
| Total | What you pay |
| Cash / Card / e-wallet | How you paid |
| Change | Cash given minus the total |

Example: items RM21.00, service charge RM2.10, SST RM1.26 gives RM24.36. Rounding takes it to **RM24.35**. If you paid RM30 cash, your change is RM5.65.

The rounding applies to the total bill, not to each item, as set out in the Bank Negara and Ministry of Domestic Trade notice. For why a bill can be higher than the menu, see [SST vs service charge](/sst-vs-service-charge).

## Five quick checks

1. **Do the items add up to the subtotal?** A wrong line is the most common mistake.
2. **Is the service charge about 10% of the subtotal?**
3. **Is the SST 6% of the subtotal?** If it is a little higher, read the article above.
4. **Is the rounding small?** It should be 1 or 2 sen at most.
5. **Does the total match what the bank or e-wallet charged you?** Compare it with the notification on your phone.

To redo the sums yourself, use the [bill calculator](/bill-calculator).

## What to keep

Keep a photo of any receipt you may need later: warranty items, anything for a claim, big purchases. For everyday meals, the number and the shop are enough.
