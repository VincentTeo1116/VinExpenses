# SST vs service charge: why your restaurant bill is higher than the menu

The menu says RM21.00. You pay RM24.35. Three things sit between those two numbers: a service charge, a tax and a rounding.

## Two different things

**Service charge** is the restaurant's own charge, usually 10%. Customs describes it as a charge added on top of the cost of the food, common in the hospitality trade.

**SST** (Sales and Service Tax) is a tax. For food and drink the service tax rate is **6%**. The general service tax rate went up to 8% on 1 March 2024, but Customs kept food and beverage at 6%. Only restaurants registered for SST, which means those whose yearly sales are above a threshold, charge it. Many small stalls don't.

## A real bill, line by line

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Nasi lemak ayam | RM12.50 |
| Teh tarik | RM3.50 |
| Roti canai × 2 | RM5.00 |
| **Subtotal** | **RM21.00** |
| Service charge 10% | RM2.10 |
| SST 6% | RM1.26 |
| Total | RM24.36 |
| Rounding | −RM0.01 |
| **You pay** | **RM24.35** |

## What the SST is calculated on

Customs' guide for the food and beverage trade says service tax is calculated on the price **excluding the service charge**. On this bill that is 6% of RM21.00 = RM1.26.

Some bills work it out differently: 6% of the food **plus** the service charge (RM23.10) gives RM1.39, which is 13 sen more. On a RM21 meal that is small. Over a big dinner it adds up.

One honest caution. The Customs guide I read is dated 23 August 2018, and I could not find a newer Customs page that changes this point. If your bill adds tax on top of the service charge, you can ask the restaurant how it is calculated. They may have a reason. I'm not a tax adviser.

## What "++" and "nett" mean

A price shown as **RM12.50++** means service charge and tax will be added to it. A price marked **nett** should already include them.

## The 5-sen rounding

Bank Negara and the Ministry of Domestic Trade brought in a 5-sen rounding for over-the-counter payments on 1 April 2008. It applies to the **total of the bill, not each item**:

| Last digit of the total | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 or 2 sen | rounds down |
| 3 or 4 sen | rounds up |
| 6 or 7 sen | rounds down |
| 8 or 9 sen | rounds up |

So RM24.36 becomes RM24.35. A total ending in 0 or 5 stays as it is. The notice does not say how the rounding line must look on a receipt, so some bills show it as an adjustment and some just show the final figure.

## Check your own bill in four steps

1. Add up the items. Does it match the subtotal?
2. Take 10% of the subtotal. Does it match the service charge?
3. Take 6% of the subtotal (not including the service charge). Does it match the SST?
4. Add everything. The total should be within 2 sen of the amount you paid.
