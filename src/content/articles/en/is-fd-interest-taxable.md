# Is fixed deposit interest taxable in Malaysia?

For most people with a fixed deposit at a Malaysian bank, no. But "most people" matters, so here is what I checked and where I stopped.

## The short answer

Interest, bonus or profit that a **resident individual** earns from deposits placed in **licensed institutions** is listed as exempt from income tax. That covers an ordinary fixed deposit at a Malaysian bank. For an Islamic term deposit the word used is *profit*, and it is included in the same wording.

If you put RM10,000 in a 12-month FD at 3.5%, you earn RM350.00. If this exemption applies to you, the whole RM350.00 is yours, with no income tax on it.

## What I checked

- The PwC Malaysia Tax Booklet lists as exempt: "Interest or bonus, gains or profits received by a resident individual from deposits placed in licensed institutions."
- YYC Advisors says in its tax Q&A that fixed deposit interest earned by an **individual** is exempt, and that a **company's** FD interest is taxable and reported as other income.

## What I could not confirm

I could not open LHDN's own page or the current text of the Income Tax Act. So I can't give you the exact paragraph of the law, and I can't tell you whether exempt interest still has to be shown in your tax return. Please ask LHDN or your tax adviser if that matters to you.

## Who this does not cover

- **Companies.** FD interest earned by a company is taxable income.
- **People who are not tax residents in Malaysia.** Different rules can apply. Check before you assume.
- **Interest from somewhere other than a licensed institution,** for example money you lend to a friend or a business. That follows different rules.

## What to do

1. Keep your bank statement or the interest advice from the bank.
2. If you are an individual and a resident, you are most likely in the exempt group, but confirm with LHDN if the amount is large or your situation is unusual.
3. If you run a company, talk to your accountant. It is taxable.

You can see how much a deposit earns with the [fixed deposit calculator](/fd-calculator). Remember the figure is the interest before any tax that might apply to you.

*General information, not tax advice. Tax rules change.*

**Sources:** PwC Malaysia Tax Booklet, "Income exempt from tax"; YYC Advisors, "Fixed deposit interest earned by company or individual".
